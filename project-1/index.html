Engineering Objectives

Why Road Condition Monitoring is Critical in India

India possesses the world's second-largest road network (over 6.3 million km). Severe monsoon downpours, overloaded commercial vehicles, and delayed maintenance cause exponential pavement deterioration and tragic road fatalities.

Monsoon Water Damage Prevention

Indian monsoons are notorious for pothole outbreaks. Standing stormwater penetrates unsealed surface cracks, saturating granular sub-bases and causing rapid pavement collapse.

Commercial Overloading Defense

Heavy commercial multi-axle freight trucks contribute high Equivalent Single Axle Loads (ESAL). Monitoring rutting depth prevents catastrophic wheelpath shearing.

Municipal Budget Optimization

Proactive surface treatment (micro-surfacing) at "Fair" condition costs ₹280/m², whereas full reconstruction of "Very Poor" roads costs ₹2,400/m²—saving municipalities up to 40%.

Eliminating Pothole Accidents

According to MoRTH accident reports, potholes cause thousands of avoidable fatalities yearly. Automated classification enables rapid emergency patching before fatal crashes occur.

Classification Categories

Road Condition Categories

Under IRC:82-2015, Indian bituminous roads are categorized into four operational condition tiers, each triggering specific maintenance interventions.

GOOD (GREEN)

Good Condition

Pavement is in sound physical condition with minimal defects. High riding comfort, excellent skid resistance, and proper drainage cross-fall.

  • Roughness (IRI): < 3.0 m/km
  • Potholes: 0 – 2 per 500m
  • Cracking Area: < 8.0%
  • Rutting Depth: < 6.0 mm
Action: Routine inspection, side drain desilting & preventive crack sealing.
FAIR (YELLOW)

Fair Condition

Moderate surface deterioration with initial bitumen binder aging, early micro-cracks, and minor wheelpath wear. Timely intervention prevents monsoon pothole propagation.

  • Roughness (IRI): 3.0 – 5.5 m/km
  • Potholes: 2 – 6 per 500m
  • Cracking Area: 8.0% – 20.0%
  • Rutting Depth: 6.0 – 12.0 mm
Action: Preventive micro-surfacing, slurry seal & localized pothole patching.
POOR (ORANGE)

Poor Condition

Significant pavement distress, extensive alligator fatigue cracks, multiple potholes, and deep rutting depressions. Ride quality and vehicle operating costs are heavily affected.

  • Roughness (IRI): 5.5 – 8.5 m/km
  • Potholes: 6 – 14 per 500m
  • Cracking Area: 20.0% – 35.0%
  • Rutting Depth: 12.0 – 20.0 mm
Action: Surface cold milling & 40mm Bituminous Concrete (BC) structural overlay.
VERY POOR (RED)

Very Poor Condition

Severe pavement disintegration, granular base failure, deep longitudinal ruts, and massive hazardous potholes. Severely compromises road safety and causes vehicle rollovers.

  • Roughness (IRI): > 8.5 m/km
  • Potholes: > 14 per 500m
  • Cracking Area: > 35.0%
  • Rutting Depth: > 20.0 mm
Action: Urgent full-depth reconstruction, FDR & subgrade geotextile stabilization.

Methodology & Algorithm (CO3)

Machine Learning Methodology

How the Random Forest Classifier was designed, trained, and verified for Civil Engineering pavement analysis.

What is Machine Learning?

Machine Learning enables computers to learn deterioration patterns directly from empirical road defect inspection data, replacing rigid empirical formulas with predictive statistical models.

Supervised Classification

Supervised Learning uses ground-truth labeled historical records to train the algorithm. Classification categorizes road segments into discrete health classes (Good, Fair, Poor, Very Poor).

Why Random Forest?

Combines an ensemble of 100 de-correlated decision trees with bootstrap aggregation. Robust against sensor noise, partitions complex interactions between distresses, and prevents overfitting.

Main Engineering Finding & Boundary Analysis (CO4 Requirement)
Our model achieved a high 95.83% accuracy on the 20% test partition. The sole misclassified sample was record IND-RD-135 (actual condition: Poor, predicted: Fair). Upon physical distress analysis, segment IND-RD-135 had an IRI of 5.4 m/km and crack extent of 18.8%, placing it directly on the boundary threshold separating Fair (IRI 3.0–5.5) and Poor (IRI 5.5–8.5) under IRC:82 guidelines. Because its rutting (12.2mm) was moderate, the ensemble majority voted Fair. This demonstrates that Machine Learning models capture the continuous, gradual nature of pavement aging rather than rigid artificial thresholds.

Maintenance Recommendations

Indian Road Maintenance Interventions (IRC:82)

Specific maintenance guidelines aligned with the Indian Roads Congress IRC:82-2015 standard maintenance manual for bituminous highways.

TIER 1 — GOOD (GREEN)

Routine Maintenance

  • Crack sealing on isolated hairline fissures (<3mm).
  • Periodic clearance of roadside storm drains and culverts.
  • Shoulder grading and vegetation clearance.
  • Biannual visual pavement condition surveys.
EST. COST: ~₹120 – ₹180 / m²
TIER 2 — FAIR (YELLOW)

Preventive Surface Treatment

  • Micro-surfacing or Slurry Seal application.
  • Rapid patch repair of isolated potholes with cold/hot mix.
  • Fog seal spraying to rejuvenate oxidized bitumen binders.
  • Rectification of shoulder drop-offs and cross-fall.
EST. COST: ~₹280 – ₹450 / m²
TIER 3 — POOR (ORANGE)

Rehabilitation & Overlay

  • Cold milling (planing) of rutted and cracked surface layer.
  • 40mm Bituminous Concrete (BC) structural overlay.
  • Deep base repair with Dense Bituminous Macadam (DBM).
  • Complete culvert and side-drain overhaul.
EST. COST: ~₹900 – ₹1,400 / m²
TIER 4 — VERY POOR (RED)

Complete Reconstruction

  • Full-Depth Reclamation (FDR) with cement/emulsion.
  • Granular subgrade stabilization and geotextile installation.
  • Full pavement redesign according to IRC:37-2018.
  • Structural drainage system reconstruction.
EST. COST: ~₹2,200 – ₹3,500 / m²

Critical Evaluation

Limitations & Future Scope

Transparent evaluation of current project boundaries and planned engineering extensions for Indian roads.

Project Limitations
  • Surface-Level Observation: The model evaluates surface distress ($IRI$, cracks, rutting, potholes) but cannot detect internal subgrade soil moisture saturation without Falling Weight Deflectometer (FWD) or Benkelman Beam Deflection (BBD) data.
  • Synthetic Academic Data: While modeled after IRC:82 and World Bank benchmarks, synthetic demonstration data cannot fully capture erratic monsoon cloudbursts or localized overloading shocks.
  • Manual Entry Subjectivity: Manual visual estimation of crack percentages and pothole counts can introduce human observational bias.
Future Engineering Scope
  • Computer Vision Pothole Detection: Deploying YOLOv8/v11 deep learning models on smartphone dashcam video to detect, measure, and geolocate potholes automatically.
  • Smartphone Accelerometer Telemetry: Using 3-axis smartphone gyroscopes and accelerometers to estimate International Roughness Index (IRI) continuously while driving.
  • Integration with PMGSY / NHAI Portals: Feeding classified health indices directly into government rural and highway asset management portals.

SECTION 1: QUESTION 1 COMPLIANCE (CO3 — 5 MARKS)

1. Problem Statement: Bituminous highway and urban road pavements across India degrade non-linearly under heavy commercial vehicular axle loads (ESAL) and monsoon downpours. Traditional visual inspections are slow, subjective, and reactive. RoadVision AI builds a machine learning classification engine that maps 7 physical inspection parameters into actionable road health categories to enable predictive intervention before structural collapse occurs.

2. Dataset Source & Engineering Benchmarks: 120 usable road records generated according to Indian Roads Congress IRC:82-2015 (Maintenance of Bituminous Surfaces) and World Bank HDM-4 IRI benchmarks across 10 Indian cities (Pune, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad, Chennai, Jaipur, Ahmedabad, Lucknow, Nagpur).

3. Input Variables & Units:

  • International Roughness Index (IRI): m/km (Bump integrator / Laser profilometer)
  • Pothole Count: count per 500m segment (visual / grid count)
  • Crack Percentage: % surface area affected by fatigue/longitudinal cracking
  • Rutting Depth: mm under 3m straight-edge gauge
  • Pavement Surface Age: years elapsed since last bituminous overlay
  • Traffic Volume: Average Annual Daily Traffic (vehicles/day)
  • Drainage Condition: Categorical rating (Good, Moderate, Poor)

4. Expected Output: 4 condition categories defined by IRC:82: Good (Green, sound pavement), Fair (Yellow, preventative repair needed), Poor (Orange, rehabilitation/overlay needed), and Very Poor (Red, urgent reconstruction).

5. Machine Learning Method Selection: Random Forest Classifier (100 trees, max depth 6). Combines ensemble bagging with random feature subspace sampling, partitions non-linear defect synergies, prevents overfitting, and outputs feature importances.

6. Train/Test Split: Exactly 80% Training (96 records) and 20% Testing (24 records) using stratified sampling.

SECTION 2: QUESTION 2 COMPLIANCE (CO4 — 5 MARKS)

1. Model Evaluation Metrics (Evaluated on Unseen 20% Test Partition):

  • Overall Test Accuracy: 95.83% (23 out of 24 test segments classified correctly)
  • Weighted Precision: 96.43%
  • Weighted Recall: 95.83%
  • Weighted F1-Score: 95.80%

2. Confusion Matrix (24 Test Samples):

3. Main Finding: Roughness Index (IRI at 25.58%) and Surface Crack Percentage (23.03%) account for 48.61% of the classification decision weight. Pavements enter structural distress rapidly when crack density exceeds 20% and IRI surpasses 5.5 m/km.

4. One Limitation: The model relies strictly on visible functional surface parameters ($IRI$, cracks, rutting) and lacks non-destructive subsurface deflection data (such as Falling Weight Deflectometer or Benkelman Beam Deflection testing) to detect underlying subgrade moisture saturation.

5. One Practical Civil Engineering Use: Municipal Pavement Management Systems (PMS). Municipalities (e.g. PMC, BMC, BBMP) can prioritize preventive maintenance on 'Fair' condition roads at ₹280/m² before they deteriorate into ₹2,400/m² structural reconstructions, saving up to 40% of annual road budgets.
